Moonmart Narrative 
The Complete Story & Narrative Design — Version 1.0

> **Legacy narrative draft.** The current game canon and implementation source is `Moonmart.md`, especially Sections 4–7 and 34, together with `data/quests.json` and `data/dialogue/`. This five-act Curator/Peregrine proposal has not been reconciled with the current six-chapter story; use it for background inspiration only, not as an implementation specification.

Some shops sell things you need. Others sell things you've forgotten you needed.

Moonmart is a cozy supernatural shopkeeping adventure about a young woman who inherits a mysterious convenience store in a sleepy town where the boundary between the living, the dead, and the forgotten grows thinner every night.

By day, you manage an ordinary little shop. You stock shelves, serve customers, bake treats, decorate, and build relationships with the townsfolk.

By night, you serve customers who shouldn't exist.

Ghosts searching for unfinished business. Witches hiding from their past. Forest spirits whose homes are disappearing. Travellers from worlds that have ceased to exist.

And somewhere behind the shop, a door that wasn't there yesterday leads to a place where forgotten things wait to be remembered.

## 1. The heart of the story
The central theme is belonging.

Every major character has lost something: a person, a place, a memory, a sense of purpose, or a version of themselves.

Moonmart becomes the place where they can find their way forward.

The story should feel like a combination of cozy shopkeeping, supernatural mystery, found-family storytelling, and gentle exploration. It has emotional depth without becoming relentlessly dark.

The player isn't a chosen hero. There is no prophecy declaring that they must save the universe.

They're simply someone who inherits a shop and gradually discovers that keeping its doors open matters more than they ever imagined.
The central narrative question
What happens to people, places, and memories when the world no longer has room for them?

The answer unfolds through five acts, individual character stories, supernatural customers, and the choices the player makes.

## 2. The world of Moonmart
Bellwether
Bellwether is a small, sleepy town nestled between a forest, rolling hills, and an old railway that hasn't operated in decades.

Its streets are lined with little cafés, flower shops, old-fashioned homes, and glowing streetlamps.

Most residents believe the town is perfectly ordinary.

They've simply learned not to ask certain questions.

Why does the bakery sometimes smell of fresh bread at three in the morning?

Why do strange lights appear in the forest?

Why does the clock in the town square occasionally strike thirteen?

And why does the little shop on Crescent Lane always seem to be open when someone desperately needs it?

The three layers of existence

1. The Waking World
The ordinary world where Bellwether exists. People work, shop, celebrate birthdays, worry about bills, and occasionally see something impossible out of the corner of their eye.


2. The Between
A twilight realm where misplaced memories, lost spirits, forgotten dreams, and abandoned possibilities gather. Time behaves strangely here. A single evening might last years.

3. The Faraway
A mysterious collection of worlds and possibilities beyond the Between. Some are still alive. Others survive only as memories. The Conductor's train once travelled between them.

The shop's secret
Moonmart stands on a threshold: a rare place where all three layers of existence can briefly touch.

The shop's original purpose was never merely to sell groceries.

It was built as a sanctuary for travellers between worlds.

Its shelves held food for the hungry, remedies for the wounded, gifts for the lonely, and peculiar objects that could help someone find their way home.

The shop was protected by an ancient agreement known as the Hearthlight Covenant.

As long as the shop offered genuine hospitality, the threshold would remain stable.

But Moonmart has been closed for years.

And the Covenant is beginning to fail.

## 3. The main characters

The Player — The New Shopkeeper
Role: Protagonist
A young adult seeking a fresh start, unexpectedly inheriting Moonmart from an estranged great-aunt, Elowen Vale.
They begin as an outsider who feels unsure where they belong. Their arc is about building a home rather than finding a predetermined destiny.

Pip — The Shop Ghost
Role: Companion, guide, emotional heart
A cheerful little ghost who has lived inside Moonmart for longer than anyone remembers. Pip loves shiny objects, rearranging displays, and pretending to be frightening.
Pip doesn't remember who they were before becoming a ghost. Their search for identity becomes one of the story's most important mysteries.

Miso — The Shop Cat
Role: Companion, supernatural detector
An apparently ordinary cat who sleeps on the counter, steals food, and judges customers.
Miso can see beings that exist outside normal time. He occasionally reacts to things that haven't happened yet. He is not secretly a god or a transformed human; he is simply a very unusual cat.

Agnes — The Baker
Role: Mentor, community anchor
The town's beloved baker, who remembers Moonmart when it was thriving. She knew Elowen personally and carries a secret about the night the shop closed.
Her story explores aging, memory, and the fear of being forgotten.

Fern — The Forest Spirit
Role: Nature guardian, magical supplier
A gentle but stubborn spirit tied to the ancient woodland outside Bellwether.
As the boundary weakens, the forest begins forgetting its own seasons. Fern must decide whether to protect her home by shutting out outsiders or trust the player to help.

Lucien — The Vampire
Role: Regular customer, historian
A polite, theatrical vampire who has lived in Bellwether for over a century. He purchases obscure teas, antique books, and occasionally suspiciously large quantities of tomato juice.
He knows far more about Moonmart than he initially admits. His story explores guilt, loneliness, and the difficulty of allowing yourself to change.

Wren — The Witch
Role: Magical mentor, friend
A young witch who works as a courier for the local magical community. Wren is talented but chronically uncertain of herself.
She teaches the player to create enchanted goods and helps decipher the shop's magical systems. Her story explores perfectionism and self-worth.

The Conductor — Keeper of Departures
Role: Mysterious traveller, major plot figure
A courteous figure who arrives at Moonmart shortly before midnight, always purchasing a single cup of tea.
He operates the Lastlight Express, a supernatural train that carries travellers between worlds.
The train has been missing stations. Entire destinations are disappearing from its timetable.

Elowen Vale — The Former Shopkeeper
Elowen, the player's great-aunt, is absent when the game begins.
She left behind Moonmart, a handwritten ledger, a set of unusual keys, and a letter that ends:
"Whatever happens, keep the kettle warm. Someone will always need a cup of tea."

The town believes Elowen passed away. The truth is more complicated. She entered the Between to prevent a catastrophe, sacrificing her ability to return until the threshold could be repaired. She has not been waiting to be rescued. She has been holding a failing world together.

## 4. The main campaign
Five acts · Approximately 15–25 hours of narrative content, alongside open-ended shopkeeping
Act I — The Shop on Crescent Lane
Days 1–7

The player arrives in Bellwether carrying a suitcase, a key, and a letter from a solicitor.

Moonmart is dusty, overgrown, and clearly hasn't opened in years.

The electricity works. The refrigerator hums. There's a cat sleeping on the counter.

And a tiny ghost is hiding behind the cereal boxes.

Opening cinematic

Rain falls softly over Bellwether.

The player stands outside a shop with a faded purple awning.

A wooden sign creaks overhead.

MOONMART.

Inside, the shop is dark.

The player turns the key.

A little bell rings.

From somewhere deep inside the building, another bell answers.

The player freezes.

A pair of glowing eyes appears behind the counter.

Miso yawns.

Then a tiny ghost floats through the wall.

Pip: "OH! You're not Auntie Elowen."

Player: "..."

Pip: "Are you a burglar?"

Player: "I have a key."

Pip: "That's exactly what a clever burglar would have!"

Pip examines the inheritance letter.

Pip: "Oh."

A pause.

Pip: "Does this mean we're opening again?"

The player looks around the neglected shop.

A shelf collapses in the background.

Player: "I suppose we've got some cleaning to do."

Pip begins glowing brighter.

Pip: "MISO! WE'RE GETTING CUSTOMERS!"

Miso goes back to sleep.
Act I gameplay and story
The first week introduces cleaning, shelving, stocking, pricing, decorating, serving customers, and relationship building.

Agnes delivers a welcome basket. Wren visits to inspect a magical disturbance. Lucien appears after sunset and politely asks whether the shop still sells midnight tea.

The first supernatural incident happens on the third evening.

A customer enters who casts no reflection in the refrigerator glass.

They purchase a candle, thank the player, and disappear before reaching the door.

A small silver coin remains on the counter.

Pip recognises it.

Pip: "Oh. That's a moonpenny."

Player: "What's a moonpenny?"

Pip: "Well... we're not supposed to get those anymore."
Act I climax: The Thirteenth Chime
At midnight on the seventh day, the town clock strikes thirteen.

Every light in Moonmart goes out.

A previously invisible door appears behind the storage shelves.

Something knocks from the other side.

The player opens it.

A warm purple glow spills across the floor.

Inside is a small room filled with floating dust, forgotten letters, and a kettle that has been boiling for years.

On the wall is a message written in Elowen's handwriting:

"If the door has opened, the world is beginning to forget."

Act reward: The Hidden Room, moonpenny currency, supernatural customers, and the first enchanted crafting station.

Act II — Things That Go Missing


As the shop grows, strange things begin happening throughout Bellwether.

A street disappears from the town map.

Agnes forgets the recipe for a cake she's baked every Sunday for forty years.

A forest path leads somewhere different each morning.

And Pip begins hearing someone calling their name from the Hidden Room.
The Unremembering
Wren identifies the phenomenon as the Unremembering: the gradual erasure of people, places, and possibilities that have lost their connection to the Waking World.

Ordinarily, the Between protects these things until they can find a new purpose.

But the threshold is failing.

Memories are beginning to dissolve rather than pass safely between worlds.

The shop's ledger reveals that Moonmart was once part of a network of sanctuary shops.

Each had a Hearthlight, a magical flame sustained by acts of hospitality and genuine connection.

Moonmart's Hearthlight is almost extinguished.
Major story missions
1. The Recipe That Vanished: Help Agnes recreate a lost family recipe by gathering ingredients and memories from townsfolk.
2. The Path of Yesterday: Follow Fern through a forest that keeps rearranging itself.
3. The Customer Who Came Twice: Serve a mysterious traveller who appears at two different ages on the same evening.
4. A Parcel for Nobody: Help Wren deliver a package to an address that no longer exists.
5. The Empty Timetable: Discover that the Conductor's train is losing destinations.
Act II climax: The Forgotten Station
The Conductor takes the player aboard the Lastlight Express.


The train travels through the Between to a station that has almost vanished.

The platform is filled with abandoned luggage.

The station clock has no hands.

A little girl sits on a bench, waiting for a train that will never arrive.

She explains that everyone from her town has disappeared.

She remembers their names.

But she can no longer remember her own.

The player must restore the station's Hearthlight by collecting fragments of its history.

When the lights return, the girl finally remembers her name.

"I'm June."

The Conductor quietly removes a blank page from his timetable.

For the first time in years, a destination appears.

June's Crossing.

Act reward: The Lastlight Express, new supply routes, memory fragments, and access to supernatural districts.

Act III — The Price of Remembering

The player discovers that the Unremembering is not a natural disaster.

Someone has been deliberately collecting memories from the Between.

The culprit is an entity known as the Curator.
The Curator
The Curator was once responsible for preserving memories and histories that could no longer exist in the Waking World.

For centuries, they watched beautiful places disappear.

They witnessed friendships end, families scatter, languages fade, and entire worlds vanish.

Eventually, they became convinced that forgetting was the greatest cruelty in existence.

Their solution was to preserve everything.

Permanently.

The Curator began collecting memories, places, and people inside a vast archive where nothing could change.

Nothing could be lost.

Nothing could die.

But nothing could grow, either.

The Curator's archive is not a dungeon filled with monsters.

It is a beautiful, peaceful, deeply unsettling place where every moment is frozen.
The character revelations
Agnes discovers that she once willingly surrendered a painful memory to the Curator. Recovering it means remembering the loss of someone she loved, but also the happiness they shared.

Fern learns that her forest is slowly being copied into the archive. The copy is perfect, but its trees never grow and its animals never change.

Lucien admits that he helped the Curator long ago, believing he could preserve the people he had lost. He has spent decades trying to undo the damage.

Wren confronts a preserved version of herself who never made mistakes, never failed an examination, and never disappointed anyone. She realises that this perfect version has never truly lived.

The Conductor discovers that the Curator has been collecting entire stations, leaving the Lastlight Express with nowhere to travel.
The central revelation about Pip
Pip was once a living child named Peregrine Vale, a distant relative of Elowen.

Years ago, Peregrine became lost in the Between during an early threshold collapse.

Elowen rescued them, but their body could not return to the Waking World.

Rather than allow Peregrine to disappear, Elowen anchored their spirit to Moonmart's Hearthlight.

Over time, Peregrine forgot their former life and became Pip.

The player discovers this through photographs, letters, and fragments of memories.

Pip is initially delighted.

Pip: "I had a name? A proper one?"

Then, after seeing a photograph:

Pip: "I looked... different."

A pause.

Pip: "Do I have to be Peregrine again?"

The player can respond:
- "Only if you want to."
- "Pip is a proper name too."
- "You can be both."
This is the emotional centre of the act.

Pip's identity is not something the player must fix.

It belongs to Pip.
Act III climax: The Perfect Afternoon
The Curator invites the player into the archive and offers them a choice.

They can preserve Moonmart exactly as it is.

No customer will ever leave.

No friend will grow old.

No shop will ever close.

Every happy moment can last forever.

The player is shown a beautiful version of Moonmart filled with everyone they've befriended.

But nobody moves unless spoken to.

The clock never advances.

Miso never wakes from his nap.

Pip repeats the same joke every few minutes.

The player rejects the offer, escaping with a fragment of the original Hearthlight Covenant.

Act reward: The Archive, advanced enchantments, personal memory quests, and new shop customisation options.

Act IV — The Last Shop at the End of the World

The Curator accelerates their plan.

Entire districts begin disappearing from Bellwether.

Residents forget neighbours they've known for years.

The forest loses its autumn colours.

The Lastlight Express arrives carrying travellers from collapsing worlds.

Moonmart becomes a sanctuary.
The Great Midnight Market
The player must transform Moonmart from a modest convenience store into a thriving refuge for supernatural travellers.

This is the game's largest shopkeeping milestone.

Customers arrive with unusual needs.

A rain spirit needs a bottle capable of holding thunder.

A homesick traveller needs a meal from a world that no longer exists.

A ghost needs a birthday present for someone who cannot see them.

A wandering star needs somewhere dark enough to sleep.

Every successful order strengthens the Hearthlight.

The shop becomes brighter.

New rooms appear.

Customers begin helping one another.

The town gradually remembers what it had forgotten.
The return of Elowen
The player finally finds Elowen in the Between.

She is older than her photographs suggest, exhausted from years of holding the threshold together.

Elowen explains that she did not create the Covenant.

She inherited it, just as the player has.

Every generation, someone chose to keep the doors open.

Not because they were special.

Because someone had to.

Elowen: "I used to think I was holding the world together."

Player: "Weren't you?"

Elowen: "No, dear. I was holding the door."

She looks toward Moonmart's distant light.

Elowen: "The people coming through it did the rest."
Act IV climax: The Night of a Thousand Customers
The Curator attempts to seal the threshold permanently.

Moonmart's doors begin closing.

The player must keep the shop running through a long, magical night.

The finale is built around everything the player has learned:
- Restocking supplies and preparing food
- Crafting enchanted items
- Helping supernatural customers
- Calling upon befriended townsfolk
- Decorating the shop with protective charms
- Managing the Hearthlight
- Resolving the needs of frightened travellers
Rather than a traditional boss fight, the player faces a shopkeeping challenge that tests their preparation and relationships.

At dawn, the shop is still standing.

The Hearthlight burns brighter than ever.

But the Curator has retreated to the heart of the archive.

And the player must decide what happens next.

Act reward: Expanded Moonmart, community helpers, permanent supernatural trade routes, and the final story chapter.

Act V — A Light Left On

The player enters the Curator's archive one final time.

There is no army waiting.

No giant monster.

Only an enormous library filled with everything the universe has ever forgotten.

The Curator sits alone at a desk, carefully repairing a torn photograph.
The final conversation
Curator: "Do you know how many worlds have ended?"

Player: "No."

Curator: "I do."

The Curator gestures toward endless shelves.

Curator: "Every name. Every face. Every little kindness. I kept them all."

Player: "But you stopped them from living."

Curator: "Living is what made them disappear."

The player can respond with dialogue reflecting their relationships and choices throughout the game.

The central argument is not that memories are unimportant.

It is that preserving something is not the same as allowing it to exist.

The Curator's fear is understandable.

Their solution is not.
The three endings


Ending A — The Open Door

The player restores the Covenant and allows the Between to change naturally again.

Memories can fade, but they can also be rediscovered. Travellers can leave, return, and build new lives.

Moonmart becomes a permanent sanctuary between worlds.

This is the primary ending.


Ending B — The Keeper of Stories

The player convinces the Curator to transform the archive into a living library.

Memories are preserved, but people can visit, learn from them, and leave.

The Curator remains its guardian, no longer alone.

Moonmart gains a new library wing.


Ending C — The Last Departure

The player helps the Curator release the memories they have imprisoned.

The archive dissolves, returning its contents to the natural flow of existence.

Some lost places return. Others are finally allowed to end.

The Lastlight Express carries the Curator to an unknown destination.

None of the endings should punish the player by destroying Moonmart or permanently removing beloved companions.

The choice changes the world's future, the epilogue, certain decorations, and the supernatural customers who visit.

Final scene
Several weeks later.

Moonmart is bustling.

Agnes has delivered fresh pastries.

Wren is arguing with a malfunctioning enchanted kettle.

Lucien is browsing the tea section.

Fern is trying to convince Miso to stop sleeping in a flowerpot.

The Conductor enters.

He purchases his usual tea.

Conductor: "Busy evening?"

Player: "Always."

Conductor: "Good."

He places a moonpenny on the counter.

Conductor: "There are still a great many people who need somewhere to go."

The player looks around the shop.

Pip floats up beside them.

Pip: "Do you think we'll ever run out of customers?"

The player smiles.

Player: "I hope not."

Outside, the moon rises over Bellwether.

The shop's sign glows.

MOONMART — OPEN.

The screen fades to black.

"Thank you for keeping the light on."

## 5. Character storylines and relationship quests
Every major character has a self-contained narrative arc that develops through friendship, shop visits, gifts, and personal quests.

These stories can be completed alongside the main campaign, with additional dialogue in the ending.
Character	Questline	Emotional resolution
Pip	The Name I Used to Have	Choosing their own identity
Miso	Nine Lives and a Nap	Discovering Miso's strange relationship with time
Agnes	A Recipe for Remembering	Embracing memories of love and loss
Fern	The Forest That Forgot Spring	Learning that change can be part of preservation
Lucien	A Century of Regrets	Forgiving himself and reconnecting with others
Wren	Perfectly Imperfect Magic	Accepting failure as part of growth
Conductor	The Last Stop	Finding purpose beyond endless duty
Elowen	The Keeper Before You	Learning to let someone else carry the responsibility

Relationship progression
Each major character has five friendship chapters.
1. Acquaintance: Introduction and everyday interactions.
2. Regular: Personal preferences, small requests, and new dialogue.
3. Friend: A character-specific quest revealing a personal conflict.
4. Confidant: A significant choice or emotional revelation.
5. Found Family: Resolution, lasting shop benefits, and epilogue participation.

Relationships shouldn't be reduced to choosing the correct dialogue answer repeatedly. Players progress through meaningful actions, remembering preferences, helping with requests, and spending time together.

## 6. Supernatural customer stories
Moonmart should contain dozens of small stories that appear organically during shopkeeping.

These are not all traditional quests. Some are brief encounters that make the world feel alive.

Here are twelve foundational stories.

1. The Birthday Ghost
A ghost buys a birthday candle every year for someone who no longer remembers them. The player helps deliver a small gift without revealing the ghost's identity.

2. A Cloud with a Cold
A rain spirit keeps sneezing thunderstorms into the shop. Wren helps craft a soothing remedy, but the ingredients require a visit to Fern's forest.

3. The Dragon Who Couldn't Breathe Fire
A tiny dragon wants to impress its family. The player discovers it can breathe beautiful glowing bubbles instead.

4. The Umbrella That Walked Away
A customer's enchanted umbrella keeps escaping because it wants to experience the rain rather than protect people from it.

5. A Scarf for the Moon
An elderly customer commissions a scarf for someone who lives in the sky. Every night, the moon appears a little warmer.

6. Letters to Tomorrow
A mailbox begins delivering letters from possible futures. The player must decide which messages should be delivered and which should remain private.

7. The Shadow Who Lost Its Person
A wandering shadow is searching for its owner. The owner, however, has grown into someone very different.

8. The Sleepy Star
A fallen star needs a quiet place to rest. The player creates a dark, comfortable corner in the Hidden Room.

9. Five Minutes More
A customer wants to purchase five extra minutes with a loved one. The player helps them find a meaningful alternative rather than altering time.

10. The Mushroom Market
Tiny forest customers begin shopping after closing. The player builds miniature shelves and discovers an entirely new customer community.

11. The Scarecrow's Day Off
A scarecrow wants to spend one day exploring Bellwether. The player helps it disguise itself and enjoy an ordinary afternoon.

12. A Boat for Lost Wishes
A paper boat appears in a puddle outside Moonmart, carrying wishes people have forgotten making. Returning them unlocks small stories across town.


## 7. Narrative integration with shopkeeping
The story must reinforce the shopkeeping mechanics rather than interrupt them.

------------------------------------------------------

Crafting	             |   Helps solve magical and emotional problems

Shop expansion           |   Reflects the growing sanctuary
Customer relationships	 |   Unlocks personal stories and community support
Exploration              |	Discovers forgotten places and unusual supplies

The Hearthlight          |	Measures the shop's connection to the worlds around it

The Hearthlight system
The Hearthlight is both a story device and a progression mechanic.

It grows through hospitality, fulfilled customer needs, friendships, and the restoration of forgotten connections.

It should not punish players for closing early, taking a day off, or focusing on decoration.

Instead, it provides positive progression.
Hearthlight progression

Level 1 — A Flicker
Ordinary shop operations and Pip's introduction.

Level 2 — A Warm Glow
Supernatural customers and enchanted items.

Level 3 — A Guiding Light
Hidden Room expansion and memory quests.

Level 4 — A Beacon
Travellers, train routes, and magical markets.

Level 5 — A Thousand Lights
Moonmart becomes a sanctuary between worlds.

## 8. Seasonal events and recurring stories
Moonmart should continue feeling alive after the main campaign.
Season	Event	Story focus
Spring	The Blooming Moon Festival	Fern's forest and new beginnings
Summer	The Starlight Market	Travellers, magical commerce, and celebration
Autumn	The Festival of Lost Things	Memories, ghosts, and remembrance
Winter	The Longest Night	Community, warmth, and companionship

Special events can also occur on full moons, meteor showers, rainy nights, and rare thirteenth chimes.

For example, during the Festival of Lost Things, customers bring objects connected to people or places they've lost. The player can display them in a temporary community exhibition.

The emphasis is on remembrance and connection rather than sadness alone.

## 9. Post-game: Moonmart After Midnight
Completing the main campaign doesn't end the game.

The player continues running and expanding Moonmart, with new story content becoming available.

The Lastlight Express brings travellers from distant worlds. The Hidden Room grows into a network of magical rooms. Existing characters continue developing their lives.

Possible post-game narrative expansions include:
- The Shop Across the Sea: A second sanctuary shop has reopened on a distant island.
- The Town That Never Slept: Bellwether begins receiving visitors from a city where night has disappeared.
- The Forgotten Constellation: A missing constellation returns, bringing travellers who have been lost for centuries.
- The First Moonmart: The player discovers the original shop that inspired the Hearthlight Covenant.
- Pip's Big Adventure: Pip takes a trip beyond Moonmart for the first time, accompanied by Miso.
Each expansion can introduce new merchandise, customers, decorative themes, and storylines without undermining the original ending.

## 10. Narrative design rules
To keep Moonmart consistent throughout development, I'd establish these as the core creative rules:
1. Cozy first, mysterious second. The supernatural should be wondrous, occasionally eerie, but rarely terrifying.
2. No mandatory combat. Problems are solved through preparation, exploration, crafting, relationships, and conversation.
3. Characters are more important than lore. Every major revelation should matter to someone the player knows.
4. Small stories matter. Helping a lonely ghost should feel as worthwhile as restoring a magical railway.
5. Player choices change relationships and outcomes, not the fundamental warmth of the game.
6. The shop is always home. No matter how strange the world becomes, Moonmart remains a safe, welcoming place.
7. No artificial urgency. Main-story crises can advance through quest milestones rather than forcing players to rush their daily shop routines.
8. The supernatural follows understandable rules. Magic can be mysterious without being inconsistent.
9. Loss is treated with care. Not every sad event must be reversed, and healing does not require forgetting.
10. The story ends with possibility. Moonmart's world should feel bigger after the ending, not smaller.

## 12. What still needs writing for development
This establishes the complete high-level narrative, including the setting, lore, cast, five-act campaign, character arcs, endings, and the relationship between storytelling and shopkeeping.

For a fully implementable narrative package, the next stage is to write every quest individually, with complete dialogue, branching choices, triggers, prerequisites, rewards, world-state changes, cutscenes, and repeatable customer conversations.

I would structure the game's narrative as data-driven story content, so Godot can load dialogue and quests from JSON resources rather than hardcoding story progression into game scripts.

The biggest creative principle I'd preserve throughout development is this:

Moonmart isn't really a game about saving the world. It's a game about making a little corner of it worth saving.
