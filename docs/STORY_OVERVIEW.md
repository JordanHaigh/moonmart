# Moonmart — Story Bible Overview

## Premise
Bellwether is an ordinary small town by day. At midnight, the boundary with a mysterious realm called the Between becomes thin. Moonmart, a convenience store, is a sanctuary for lost travelers crossing between worlds. The player works overnight, serves customers, befriends locals and uncovers why the old protective agreement is failing.

## Tone
Heartwarming and bittersweet. No combat, jump scares, permanent missed essential stories, or catastrophic financial failure. The player finds meaning in remembering people and their routines.

## World
- Moonmart: retail floor, backroom, tea corner, reading nook, greenhouse, hidden midnight room.
- Old Bellwether: bakery, bookshop, town square.
- Whisperwood: shifting paths, ancient grove and forest spirits.
- Moonwater Harbour: impossible arrivals and forgotten objects.
- Old Lighthouse: light guiding travelers through the Between.
- Midnight Platform: railway station not found on maps.

## Main cast
- Miso: orange-and-cream shop cat and guardian of the threshold.
- Pip: cheerful ghost who buys strawberry milk and searches for lost memories.
- Agnes Thorne: baker who knows the town's hidden history.
- Fern: shy forest spirit whose grove is threatened by silence.
- Lucien Vale: vampire librarian struggling with changing friendships.
- Wren Hollow: traveling witch searching for a missing passenger.
- The Conductor: remembers those forgotten by everyone else.
- Elias Marsh: last lighthouse keeper.

## Six chapters
1. The First Night — learn the shop, meet Miso, Agnes and Pip.
2. Things That Don't Belong — magical visitors, products and Whisperwood.
3. The Forgotten Town — photographs change and the Long Midnight is revealed.
4. The Midnight Train — discover the station and Miso's history.
5. The Last Light — restore the lighthouse and uncover the old agreement.
6. Until Morning Comes — decide the future of the crossing.

## Endings
The Keeper: maintain the agreement.
The Open Door: create open coexistence between worlds.
The Gentle Goodbye: help the lost move on and close the original doorway.
All three are meaningful, not punitive.
