# MOONMART — STORY BIBLE v0.1

## Logline
You take the night shift at a sleepy corner shop on the edge of Bellwether. Ordinary customers come for milk and biscuits. Others arrive from the Between, a place made of lost memories and forgotten roads. When the boundary begins to fade, your little shop may be the last place where everyone is still remembered.

## Emotional thesis
To remember someone is to make a place for them in the world. Small acts of kindness can matter as much as grand gestures. The tone is heartwarming and bittersweet: quiet loneliness, gentle humour, grief without cruelty, and endings that offer hope without erasing loss.

## Locked world rules
- Bellwether is a small forest-and-harbour town, ordinary by day.
- At midnight, the boundary with the Between thins.
- The Between contains lost places, memories, dreams, spirits and forgotten travellers.
- Names, promises and memory have supernatural power.
- Moonmart stands above a threshold; its original agreement guaranteed safe passage and a place to rest.
- The Long Midnight happened decades ago: dawn did not come, people disappeared, streets shifted, and most residents forgot.
- The agreement is now weakening. The town begins forgetting people and places.
- Miso has guarded the shop for longer than an ordinary cat could live.
- The player is never forced into combat or permanently punished for a missed clue.

## Story structure
### Chapter 1 — The First Night (target shifts 1–5)
New job, Agnes's help, Miso's habits, Pip's strawberry milk, the 1974 receipt, first glimpse of a hidden door. Central question: who is Pip and why is the receipt older than the shop's current records?

### Chapter 2 — Things That Don't Belong (target shifts 6–12)
Fern, Wren and Lucien become regulars. Magical inventory becomes available. Whisperwood opens. Birdsong vanishes from the forest. The player learns that products and hospitality can anchor travellers to a place.

### Chapter 3 — The Forgotten Town (target shifts 13–20)
Photographs alter, a street is missing from maps, and residents forget neighbours. Old ledgers reveal the Long Midnight. Agnes begins revealing her former role as a keeper of the boundary.

### Chapter 4 — The Midnight Train (target shifts 21–28)
The platform appears. The Conductor remembers every passenger. Wren searches for a missing person. Miso's role becomes clearer. The player learns that the shop is a sanctuary, not merely a business.

### Chapter 5 — The Last Light (target shifts 29–36)
The abandoned lighthouse briefly lights up. Elias explains that its light guided lost travellers, not ships. The boundary frays, and the shop's original promise must be renewed or transformed.

### Chapter 6 — Until Morning Comes (target shifts 37–42)
Reconcile characters, repair or reshape the agreement, and decide the future of Moonmart. Three ending families: Keeper, Open Door, Gentle Goodbye. No ending is a punitive failure state.

## Core cast
**Miso** — Orange-and-cream shop cat. Quiet guardian; initially appears to be an ordinary pet. Communicates through habits, dreams, and eventually memory echoes. Never dies or runs away.

**Pip** — Friendly glowing ghost who comes for strawberry milk on Tuesdays. Forgotten their original name. Their arc asks whether recovering a past must erase a present identity.

**Agnes Thorne** — Warm baker and former boundary caretaker. Carries guilt for decisions made during the Long Midnight. Supports the player without taking over the store.

**Fern** — Shy forest spirit; the woods are losing birdsong. Their arc concerns care for a home that is slowly changing.

**Lucien Vale** — Exhausted vampire librarian who drinks decaf. Their arc follows an old friendship across unequal lifespans.

**Wren Hollow** — Travelling witch and magical supplier. Searches for a person who boarded the Midnight Train and never returned.

**The Conductor** — Keeper of the impossible railway platform. Remembers those everyone else has forgotten.

**Elias Marsh** — Last lighthouse keeper. Knows the light once guided lost souls and travellers to safety.

## Relationship system
Stranger → Familiar → Friend → Confidant → Kindred. Progress through meaningful interactions, remembering preferences, dialogue choices and story milestones. No repetitive daily gifting grind. Important decisions become durable memory flags.

## Mystery design principles
1. Give every major clue at least two discovery routes.
2. Never make an irreversible wrong deduction.
3. Foreshadow reveals with ordinary details.
4. Avoid sudden jump scares and cruel twists.
5. Make small observations matter: changed orders, empty seats, receipts, routines.
6. Keep the shop a safe emotional centre even when the world outside changes.

## Endings
**Keeper:** Preserve the original agreement and guard the threshold.
**Open Door:** Establish a new, more open coexistence between Bellwether and the Between.
**Gentle Goodbye:** Help travellers reach their destinations and close the threshold, leaving an ordinary but changed town.
Character epilogues depend on individual arcs and promises kept.

## Narrative dependencies
The Pip receipt mystery teaches the journal and clue-linking system. Fern's forest story teaches environmental observation. Lucien's story introduces time and memory. Wren's story leads toward the Midnight Platform. Agnes and Elias connect the Long Midnight to the original agreement. Miso's revelations tie the shop to the player's final choice.

## Narrative safeguards
Avoid implying every ghost is a dead person. The Between is not simply an afterlife. Avoid reducing grief to a puzzle with a single correct answer. The player can listen, comfort, or choose silence. Essential story progression cannot require maximizing profit.
