# Moonmart raster art pack

The visual source of truth is `art_import/`. The generated in-game artwork was authored against `I LOVE THIS.png`, `BEST ART CANDIDATE.png`, and the sleeping-cat reference in that folder. Keep new sprites in the same painted, softly textured palette and use transparent PNG layers for objects and effects. Do not replace this pack with placeholder SVG illustrations.

## Section 27: scene layering

The shop is drawn from named, independently controlled scene layers in this order:

1. Night sky and distant Bellwether parallax, visible through the fixed window.
2. Moon halo and window weather, seasonal atmosphere, and story-memory effects.
3. Architecture plate: wall, floor, trim, fixed window opening, doorway structure.
4. Background furniture, then depth-sorted characters and interactables.
5. Foreground furniture, with prop effects placed at their intended depth.
6. HUD and dialogue on the screen-space CanvasLayer.

Section 27 is the highest-priority art acceptance gate: preserve each environment plate and keep dynamic lighting and effects independently controllable. Never bake light rays or light particles into a plate, even when they appear in a composed `art_import/` reference. Animated or switchable glow, rain, snow, wind-borne leaves or motes, switchable fog, and story effects also remain separate. Fixed moons, stars, clouds, distant haze, fixture warmth, and reflections may remain as scenic painting when they do not change with weather, story state, upgrades, or accessibility settings. The moon halo is a separate `PointLight2D`; weather, memory, and seasonal atlases are separate sprites.

Treat composed `art_import/` screenshots as quality and style references; do not flatten their rays, floating particles, steam, or other animated cues into an environment PNG. Recreate those cues on separate raster layers or dedicated lights while preserving static fixture warmth, fixed moons and stars, and painted reflections.

In the runtime scene tree, `NightSkyLayer`, `ExteriorParallaxLayer`, `SeparateLightingLayer`, `WindowEffectsLayer`, `ArchitectureLayer`, `BackgroundFurnitureLayer`, `DepthSortedCharactersLayer`, `ForegroundFurnitureLayer`, `PropEffectsLayer`, and `HUDDialogueLayer` make those roles inspectable. The fixed architecture and the sky remain separate. The window panes are transparent; a pixel-only alpha cleanup removed the low-alpha red matte residue without changing any painted architecture. Dynamic effects are never merged into the architecture texture.

For every location, the sky and exterior-parallax layers can be switched independently through `location_night_sky` and `location_exterior_parallax`; those choices persist when scenes change. They remain scenery controls, separate from the visual-effects and dynamic-lighting master settings. This makes it possible to inspect each composed layer while leaving the intact architecture plate visible.

The backroom uses an explicit `ArchitectureLayer` for its intact plate, followed by separate `RoomDecorationLayer` and `DepthSortedCharactersLayer` nodes; placed furniture stays independent of the painted backroom plate and sorts against the player. Its collision layer is also separate from the artwork.

### Environment plate visual signoff

The metadata gate cannot inspect image pixels, so a `contains_vfx: false` declaration is never sufficient approval by itself. Every environment image must have a matching record in `environment_visual_review.json` with its current SHA-256, and the ledger records hashes for its `art_import/` source sheets. A separate source-integrity list pins every PNG in `art_import/`; these identity hashes do not imply visual approval. Review each image at native size and normal in-game scale against `art_import/`, checking that painterly quality and composition remain at reference quality, no light rays or particles are baked into the plate, and no changeable glow, weather effect, or story VFX is baked in. Fixed scenic moons, stars, clouds, distant haze, fixture warmth, and reflections are allowed only when they remain part of the unchanging authored scene. For separated layers, verify the intended composite and preserve unrelated painted pixels. If any image or reference sheet changes, review dependent artwork again and update its digest; `tools/validate_section27.ps1` rejects missing, stale, duplicate, untracked, or out-of-root signoffs and references.

District, story-room, and optional shop-room plates remain intact raster paintings. Their runtime scenes create or reuse sibling `SeparateLightingLayer`, `WindowEffectsLayer`, `WeatherEffectsLayer`, `StoryEffectsLayer`, and `ForegroundEffectsLayer` nodes above the plate for independently controlled effects. Creation and visibility inherit the current dynamic-lighting and visual-effects settings, including when a scene is entered after settings are changed. Local per-layer and per-effect visibility choices are retained while the master accessibility setting is off, then restored when the player enables it; the master setting always gates the result. Child lights inherit visibility from their dedicated parent lighting layer. Godot 2D lights target canvas items through their z-range and item mask, so parent-layer draw order alone does not hide light from an opaque plate; restrict the receiver range to match the composition. No scene-authored district moon halos are currently placed; runtime decoration lights remain attached to the same separate layer when a placed fixture declares one. Rainy shifts place compact animated cells from the approved rain atlas in district-specific reviewed atmospheric pockets; snowy shifts animate isolated flakes sampled from the approved seasonal atlas in those same pockets; wind uses season-matched sprites from that seasonal atlas: pink petals in spring, faint warm motes in summer, drifting leaves in autumn, and small flakes in winter. Outdoor sprite bounds remain within the scene, and snow and wind motion stays bounded around its reviewed anchor so effects do not wash across prominent foreground scenery already painted into the opaque plate. District weather uses `WeatherEffectsLayer` behind characters. Tea Corner routes pane-centered weather through its under-architecture `WindowEffectsLayer`, so the transparent pane masks keep effects inside the glass. Effects are weather/season gated and freeze in reduced-animation mode. Fog has no approved matching raster overlay yet, so the environment plates remain untouched. Keep effects below or around foreground silhouettes as appropriate; an overlay must not wash across foreground objects that are already painted into the plate. The Tea Corner keeps `moonmart_tea_corner.png` untouched as its source plate. `tools/split_tea_corner_window.ps1` extracts its existing window-view pixels into transparent `moonmart_tea_corner_night_sky.png` and `moonmart_tea_corner_exterior_parallax.png` layers, while `moonmart_tea_corner_architecture.png` clears alpha only in the pane masks. The script preserves source RGB and validates exact source-over recomposition with zero changed RGBA pixels; all frame and muntin pixels remain on the architecture layer. The transparent structural atlas is catalogued in `data/architecture_modules.json` and its pieces belong on `ArchitectureLayer`, separate from the plates. The Section 27 review covered Moonmart architecture, window sky and parallax, Old Bellwether, Whisperwood, Moonwater Harbour, Old Lighthouse, Midnight Platform, the backroom, the hidden room, and the Tea Corner. These plates remain intact: the review found no baked light rays, particle effects, rain, snow, or story VFX. Static moonlight, fixture illumination, fixed stars, and painted reflections remain part of the authored environment art; these static scene details are distinct from dynamic light particles.

Before accepting new art, run `pwsh -File tools/validate_section27.ps1` from the project root. The Section 27/28 gate requires each environment image to exist and declare `contains_vfx: false`; checks runtime environment references and SVG references; requires every PNG under `assets/raster/` to be catalogued and cite `art_import/`; hash-checks identity pins for every PNG under `art_import/`; and checks that catalogued PNGs exist. Its source sweep covers Godot scripts, textual scenes/resources/shaders, and C# sources across the project while excluding tests, QA evidence, templates, and generated caches. It checks that known runtime lights and effect sprites stay attached to gated layers, blocks unlayered light/particle nodes in scenes, and rejects new runtime light or particle constructors until their layer contract is reviewed and updated. Source identity pins do not count as visual approval. Catalogued atlases and transparent PNGs must link to existing dark-plum and warm-cream preview files. The script cannot detect painted rays or particles by itself: visually inspect every new or revised environment plate against the art-import reference and reject any baked dynamic effect.

Editable source packages live outside Godot's runtime raster folder under `art_sources/`. The Tea Corner OpenRaster file retains its exact-split architecture, sky, and exterior-parallax PNGs as separate editable layers; `tools/validate_art_sources.ps1` verifies each embedded layer and the merged plate against the approved exports byte-for-byte. Rebuild it with `pwsh -File tools/build_tea_corner_source.ps1`. This is a reconstructed editable stack, not the original painting-session file; original layered masters for the other plates remain unavailable.

## Section 28: production conventions

### Visual acceptance gate

- Use `art_import/` as the visual authority for palette, texture, form language, lighting, and finish. New raster pieces must match the references in a dark-room and a light-background preview.
- Inspect transparent pixels around each silhouette. Reject detached color specks, magenta/red/yellow matte fringes, clipped details, and opaque backgrounds. Preserve intentional soft shadows and authored glow only when they belong to the prop.
- Keep atlas cells integer-sized with transparent gutters. Record the exact grid, cell size, and frame order in the manifest and use those same values in runtime regions.
- Never repair a location plate by painting over or erasing its scenery to make room for effects. Add the effect as an independent transparent raster layer or dedicated light under the Section 27 layer contract.

All 73 catalogued atlas or transparent PNG entries now have paired dark-plum and warm-cream QA previews (146 composites total), with preview paths recorded in the manifest. QA composites, candidate-review source sheets, source-only inputs, and repair evidence live under `qa_asset_repairs/`, which contains `.gdignore` so review files are not imported as game resources. The source-only inputs used to prepare Agnes's talk pose and the two final product icon atlases are kept there; runtime uses their catalogued production outputs. The four raster effect atlases have paired composites too; their soft pale edges vary naturally with alpha and were kept unchanged after review. The shop architecture and structural atlas received pixel-only alpha cleanup; no opaque painted pixels changed. Keep effects independent from the room plate and calibrate structural placements in the room before enabling them.

The transparent shop-window Bellwether panorama used by Moonmart's interior parallax layer received a reversible alpha-only cyan-edge repair: 6,028 boundary pixels matching the recorded low-alpha color predicate were cleared while preserving RGB and all other pixels. It is not an Old Bellwether district cutout. The untouched source and dark-plum/warm-cream previews are stored in `qa_asset_repairs/` and indexed in `asset_manifest.json`.

## Typography
